Innovation scorecard. In order to demonstrate that your business idea is making progress, your team needs to show hard evidence from all four risk categories, desirability, feasibility, viability, and adaptability. But how do you evaluate if you're making progress or not? To assess if you're making progress from your initial idea to a profitable business, we developed the innovation project scorecard. It is fully aligned with the nine building blocks of the business model canvas and has been designed to help you demonstrate that you've reduced risk in the four risk categories. Let's go through them. First, we ask whether there is evidence of desirability. For example, do our critical customer segments have the jobs, pains, and gains relevant for selling our value proposition? Have we found the best channels, And did we develop the right relationship? In feasibility, we evaluate if we have the right technology and resources to create our value proposition, if we have the right capabilities to handle key activities for creating our value proposition, and if we have found the right key partners. Next up, in viability, we evaluate if we know how much our customers are willing to pay us and how they will pay and if we understand our cost structures. And finally, adaptability, where we ask ourselves if we understand the competitors and emerging players in our markets as well as market forces, key trends, and macroeconomic forces. For each of the four risk categories, you can evaluate your idea based on a point system that goes from zero to ten. Zero points if you have no evidence at all and you didn't run any experiments yet, five points if there is evidence from one experiment, and ten points if there is evidence from several experiments with increasingly stronger evidence. At the beginning of your testing journey, you really don't have a lot of evidence about whether your idea works or not. Because you haven't made much progress yet, you're likely scoring yourself quite low. As you progress your testing journey and run more experiments with increasingly stronger evidence, you should be able to score yourself higher and higher in all four risk categories, making more progress towards validated business case. There's a bit more to the innovation scorecard though. If you're an established company, you also need to evaluate the strategic fit. Meaning, is your project aligned to your company's vision and innovation guidance, and do you have leadership support? If you are a start up, you check if your business idea corresponds with your vision and values instead. And last but not least, the final section of the scorecard focuses on the potential size of the financial opportunity. The more both leaders and teams know about the value of an idea, the better decisions they can make about whether to scale or stop working on an idea. We have found the scorecard to be useful in various situations. The main use is for teams evaluating their own progress during a sprint or a weekly stand up. Are they moving the needle on their project? Coaches and consultants use the tool to benchmark an innovation project at any time Because each project has a unique pattern showing different levels of evidence for each risk category, the scorecard helps deciding what to do next. For innovation teams and organizations who are pitching their projects to leaders for investment so that a fair decision can be reached that is not based on whether leaders like or dislike the team's innovative idea. And investors can use the scorecard to decide whether an idea is worth investing in or not, all based on evidence and not an opinion or flashy looking pitches.
